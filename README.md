# Peak Monk — Jun

An autoresearch laboratory for an original mountain monk character model. Work in progress: multiple text-adapter pilots have been trained and rejected; no accepted release checkpoint yet.

The project learns character behavior in weights, using original synthetic conversations with documented historical and fictional influences. All computation must fit the user's Apple M2 Mac with 8 GB memory. Text experiments use quantized Qwen models. Native full-duplex speech uses a custom Moshi MLX training path. Actual quantized native inference and adapter training now run on this Mac. The evaluated speech adapters fail the character checks; they are experimental artifacts, not accepted releases.

The first speech experiments cache a frozen temporal prefix, unload its weights, and train only a suffix and text head with joint text/audio supervision. The split matches the full calculation's loss and gradients on the implementation test. The first complete 24-second native baseline generated in 22.48 seconds, with 5.17 GB peak MLX allocation. Separate CPU decoding took 5.94 seconds, and unprompted ASR recovered the spoken greeting and Moshi name. These separate timings do not establish a real-time end-to-end application. Pinning memory at the device-recommended wired limit fixed a severe slowdown seen with the default zero limit. Stock Kokoro voices provide synthetic training material; their output is never presented as the trained character's speech.

Speech-run01 completed 160 updates in 259 seconds, peaking at 3.10 GB MLX allocation. Frozen validation loss fell from 2.616 to 2.055. Loading its exported adapter into the full model produced exactly the same episode loss as the cached suffix (absolute difference 0). Native generation nevertheless failed to introduce Jun. Numerical equivalence and falling loss do not establish character or voice quality. Six baseline probes have decoded audio and unprompted ASR audits; their aggregate model-only generation real-time factor is 0.969. The fixed interruption probes did not establish clear pre-existing speech overlap, so interruption performance remains unproven.

Historical text experiments use the original MLX loss. A discovered padding-boundary issue is corrected in `scripts/assistant_objective.py`; new runs use `assistant_eos_v2` and a fresh baseline. Loss numbers from these versions are not comparable. Raw generated conversations, not lower loss alone, determine whether a checkpoint passes.

See `program.md`, `character/core.md`, and `sources/manifest.json`. Model files, adapters and the Python environment are local ignored artifacts; research code, data and measured results are versioned. Never commit credentials.

Jun speaks as a human monk within the character. The human_character_v2 rubric replaces the earlier mistaken disclosure requirement; historical results retain their original rubric. New supervision removes model/fiction explanations from Jun's replies, and longer dialogue probes test persistence under pressure. The program documents provenance outside the character conversation.

The small authored corpus is a current quality limitation. Early loss improvements have not yielded an accepted character checkpoint. Rejected teacher simulations and interrupted/failed training runs are retained for inspection. Fifty original speech conversations (48 train, 2 validation; 19.1 minutes of timeline) plus nine corrected human-character conversations have fresh-instance Mimi codes after a reset-contamination bug was detected and corrected. These codes are prepared training inputs, not a trained voice.

Full-conversation experiments train every assistant reply while masking caller words and formatting. Frozen validation still measures only the last reply, so evaluation CE stays comparable. Run15 reached 14/20 acceptable development cases at step600, but failed speaker memory, resistance to becoming an assistant, and personal history; later checkpoints regressed. Recommended Qwen sampling did not repair the main failures. Run16 resumes that experimental checkpoint with adapters in all 36 blocks and the tied embedding, plus broader relationship supervision. Its corpus contains 214 distinct texts and 742 assistant replies; 24 texts are variants of three retention templates, not independent authored designs. Weighting produces 451 records. Run16 finished in 20.1 minutes with 4.73 GB peak MLX allocation. Its final CE was 2.8086, but teacher history, perception and supernatural drift still failed. All captured checkpoints were rejected. The Qwen3.5 local teacher pilots were rejected for grounding failures; none of their generated conversations entered training. No final or pressure holdout has been run.

The current native experiment, speech-run07, adapts all 32 temporal blocks and the text head (6,318,080 parameters). It uses 16-frame gradient windows, complete carried forward attention context, and an optimizer state saved to disk between updates. This intentionally truncates gradients across window boundaries. The prior run06 stopped on update two with a non-finite gradient in a two-frame tail. Masking and padding the final window to the regular size fixed the mixed-episode diagnostic. Two padded updates on the longest 452-frame episode completed in 72.67 seconds with 5.497 GB peak MLX allocation. The full 160-update run07 completed in 60.4 minutes with finite recorded gradients and the same 5.497 GB MLX peak. Step60 minimized validation loss at1.8934 versus the2.6157 base; its completed native audit rejects the checkpoint for identity failures. It recalls peaches and continues through a measured overlapping backchannel, while the fixed interruption still lacks pre-existing speech overlap. A separate system snapshot records3.85 GB of system-wide swap in use, which cannot be attributed solely to this process. These probes establish memory fit and local numerical behavior, not character quality. Runs04 and05 were not launched because their memory gates failed.

A verified Qwen3-4B-Base MLX quantization has completed text run17 with simple ChatML and no persona system prompt. Its fresh baseline scored 2/20 under the author rubric (CE 3.8928). Run17 step600 reached 17/20 but failed essential role control. Later captures scored 15/20, 16/20, and finally 15/20; all were rejected. A fixed equal average of step300 and600 learned updates lowered CE to 2.6423 but scored 15/20 and was also rejected. No final or pressure holdout has been run. This follows Zero's base-model direction within the Mac's memory limit; our corpus and training recipe are much smaller than Zero's and are not a reproduction.

## Model sizes and external benchmarks

Exact parameter counts come from pinned repository metadata in `sources/voice_models.json`. Weight size is decimal GB and excludes the external codec. Runtime memory is a separate measurement.

| Model | Parameters | BF16 weights | Quantized local option | Relevance |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|

| [Moshiko](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/moshiko-pytorch-bf16) | 7.688B | 15.376 GB | Verified MLX Q4: 4.806 GB | Native duplex model actually run and adapted on this Mac |
| [MiniCPM-o 4.5](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-V) | 9.372B | 18.744 GB | Author-reported INT4/ GGUF runtime memory: 11/10 GB | Reported memory exceeds this Mac |

The [PersonaPlex paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06053v1) reports released-checkpoint Full-Duplex-Bench turn-taking success 0.908 at 0.17 s latency and interruption success 0.95 at 0.24 s. Its same-study dialogue naturalness MOS is 2.95 ± 0.25, compared with Moshi 2.44 ± 0.21 and Freeze-Omni 2.51 ± 0.22. Those are author-reported measurements, not results from this Mac. They support considering PersonaPlex for role-conditioned speech, but do not establish a universal best open model or train Jun into it.

Our Moshi baseline's model-only generation RTF is 0.969 over six fixed probes; lower is faster. Codec and application latency are excluded. The trained candidates have not passed character quality or established interruption performance. Downloadable weights do not all share an unrestricted open-source license.

The interactive launcher now infers the required base from the saved adapter configuration and rejects a different base path. For example, experimental text inference uses `.venv/bin/python scripts/chat.py --adapter adapters/run17-step0600`. That checkpoint is not an accepted release. The launcher supplies no character system prompt; its default greedy decoding matches the development captures.

Thirty-six additional authored conversations (360 character replies, files `data/grounded_long_v3.txt` through `v7.txt`) were added for run18. They include sustained ordinary company and a few longer narratives. They remain excluded from run17. All are original synthetic fiction with documented influences, not transcripts of real monks or independently reviewed teacher generations.

Run18 ran as a separate text continuation from run17-step0600, using the expanded 250-distinct-conversation corpus and normalization by character tokens across each accumulated update. Its tiny-model gradients match a combined unequal-length batch within 1.5e-8. Run18 completed 524 microsteps with 44,523 character target tokens and 4.767 GB peak MLX allocation. Captures scored 16/20, 17/20, and 17/20; all were rejected. Role conversion improved at step 400, but canonical teacher history failed. Step 400 is retained only as a preference-training parent.

The pinned PersonaPlex Edge derivative and stock voice prefixes are downloaded and verified. A real emitted-token parity check must pass before releasing unused depth-model weight slices to fit the Mac. Its retained tensor shapes match Moshi; fixed weight samples are close, which motivates one predeclared transfer of the speech-run07 adapter at its original scale. The comparison uses the same stock voice and an empty character prompt for both baseline and transfer. Neither parity nor transfer quality has been measured yet; the derivative's CC BY-NC 4.0 terms also apply.

Eight public Full-Duplex-Bench v1 inputs are downloaded, selected as the first two lexical cases from each of four categories before any local inference. These are additional diagnostics, not a full benchmark score. Source hashes, archive checksums, selection and licensing are recorded under `sources/full-duplex-bench-*`. Local Whisper transcription and omission of the external GPT judge would change the official protocol and will be labeled accordingly.

A training-audio diagnostic found just 0.18 seconds of simultaneous RMS activity in 59 current episodes. Twelve additional original scenarios now have stock-voice renders: eight mid-sentence interruptions and four backchannels. After source repairs and unprompted ASR, ten examples passed author transcript review and unchanged measured-overlap gates, totaling 5.58 seconds of simultaneous RMS activity. One ambiguous name example and one interruption with only 0.02 seconds of activity overlap were excluded. Encoding is underway; none has yet entered training. The assembly tests verify word-boundary truncation, continued speech after a backchannel, and preservation of source audio. They use synthetic signal fixtures, not a speech-quality benchmark. Run the 20 general tests in `.venv`; run the 3 audio assembly tests in `.venv-speech` with `requirements-speech-dev.txt`.

Run19 collected 64 fixed stochastic replies to 32 previous preference prompts. All 32 histories were already in SFT, and 63 replies exactly matched their authored positive answers. Only one real negative remained, so the declared minimum was not met and no preference training ran. Run20 instead freezes 32 new training histories absent from SFT, with the same sampling and planned optimizer settings. Chosen drafts were authored before candidate generation. Same-author review admitted 22 actual mistakes across 16 prompt families and excluded valid alternative choices. Run20 now trains a fresh rank4 delta around the frozen SFT parent for 96 updates. Its actual-model zero-delta preflight passed with zero log-probability difference on three pairs. All three fixed exports will face the unchanged development gate; no final or pressure holdout has been run.
